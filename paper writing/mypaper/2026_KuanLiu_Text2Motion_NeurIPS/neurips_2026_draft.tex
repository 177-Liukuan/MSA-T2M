\begin{abstract}
Text-driven human motion generation in continuous latent spaces faces a fundamental trade-off: while semantic alignment is essential for controllability, forcing motion manifolds toward text distributions often distorts physical structures, leading to a decline in generation realism. To resolve this, we propose MSA-T2M, a framework that treats multi-scale semantic alignment and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) as a tightly coupled solution. We first introduce MSA-VAE, a causal variational autoencoder featuring dual-track physical-semantic disentanglement. By performing joint local (frame-level) and global (sequence-level) alignment, MSA-VAE organizes the latent space into a semantically discriminative and physically consistent structure. We then identify the alignment-realism tension---where tighter alignment improves R-Precision but degrades FID---and propose RAG-Diffusion-AR to bridge this gap. By retrieving real motion priors from the newly structured latent bank, our autoregressive diffusion Transformer anchors the generation back to the natural motion manifold. Experiments on the HumanML3D-272 benchmark demonstrate that MSA-T2M achieves state-of-the-art FID and R-Precision. Our results validate that multi-scale alignment creates a superior retrieval index, while RAG effectively compensates for the realism loss inherent in cross-modal alignment, offering a new paradigm for high-fidelity, semantically precise motion synthesis.
Code will be publicly available upon acceptance.
\end{abstract}



\section{Introduction}

Text-driven human motion generation (T2M) plays a central role in applications such as virtual reality, animation, and embodied AI \citep{Guo_2022_CVPR,tevet2022human,xiao2025motionstreamer}.
Recent advances have progressively shifted from discrete VQ-VAE-based tokenization \citep{zhang2023generating} to continuous latent-space generation \citep{meng2024rethinking,meng2025absolute,xiao2025motionstreamer}, where causally-structured compact representations better preserve motion fidelity and enable high-quality sequential synthesis.
However, these latent spaces are optimized exclusively for motion reconstruction: without explicit semantic organization, text-motion alignment is delegated to the second-stage generator and degrades sharply for complex or compositional descriptions.

While MotionCLIP \citep{tevet2022motionclip} pioneered cross-modal alignment by pulling motion encoder outputs toward CLIP text embeddings, subsequent state-of-the-art methods \citep{tevet2022human,chen2023executing,zhang2023generating,xiao2025motionstreamer} gradually abandoned explicit latent-space alignment, delegating text-motion correspondence entirely to the second-stage generator.
The few works that revisit latent-space alignment \citep{maldonado2025moclip, he2025molingo} impose only single-scale, frame-level local constraints---missing a more fundamental observation: \emph{text-motion alignment is inherently multi-scale}.
Short motion segments carry atomic semantics (e.g., \textit{raise the left arm}), while full sequences encode compositional intent (e.g., \textit{walk forward and then sit down}).
Local-only alignment captures fine-grained detail but lacks global coherence; global-only captures overall intent but misses local semantics---and no existing latent-space method achieves both.

Multi-scale alignment, however, introduces an inherent tension that prior work has not addressed (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}a).
Text embeddings occupy a fundamentally different space from physical motion dynamics; forcing motion latents toward text distributions distorts the motion manifold, degrading realism (FID) even as it improves semantic alignment (R-Precision).
\emph{The more tightly we align, the more we risk sacrificing physical plausibility---alignment alone cannot simultaneously maximize both objectives.}

This tension reveals a new role for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) \citep{lewis2020retrieval} (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}b): retrieved real motion priors are drawn directly from the natural motion distribution (physically plausible) yet semantically close to the query text, compensating for the realism loss from alignment and enabling strong FID \emph{and} R-Precision simultaneously.
However, existing RAG methods \citep{zhang2023remodiffuse,kalakonda2025morag} retrieve from raw, high-dimensional motion space, which is noisy and redundant; retrieval in a multi-scale aligned latent space---where semantically similar motions cluster tightly---yields far more discriminative priors.

In this paper, we propose \textbf{MSA-T2M}, the first framework to jointly achieve multi-scale semantic alignment in the motion latent space and leverage it for retrieval-augmented generation (see Fig.~\ref{fig:arc}).
Our key insight is that alignment and RAG form a tightly coupled solution: multi-scale alignment structures the latent space into a discriminative retrieval index, while latent-space RAG compensates for the realism-semantics trade-off that alignment inevitably introduces.
We design \textbf{MSA-VAE}, a causal VAE with dual-track physical-semantic disentanglement: local alignment matches frame-level latents to BABEL action labels \citep{punnakkal2021babel}, while global alignment anchors a \texttt{[CLS]} token---serving as the retrieval key for an offline prior bank---to dynamically interpolated HumanML3D captions \citep{Guo_2022_CVPR}, organizing the latent space at both scales without discarding physical structure.
Building on this, \textbf{RAG-Diffusion-AR} retrieves Top-$K$ semantically similar real motion priors, fuses them via softmax-weighted pooling into prefix tokens, and applies \textbf{Synergistic CFG} to jointly balance text and retrieval guidance---restoring the generation realism that alignment alone cannot sustain.

Experiments on HumanML3D-272 \citep{Guo_2022_CVPR,xiao2025motionstreamer,fan2025go} demonstrate state-of-the-art FID and competitive R-Precision, with ablations confirming that alignment and RAG contribute complementarily.

\textbf{Our contributions are summarized as follows:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item We identify that latent-space semantic alignment is inherently a multi-scale problem and propose \textbf{MSA-VAE}, a causal VAE with dual-track physical-semantic disentanglement that achieves joint local-global alignment, yielding a structured latent space that is both semantically discriminative and physically consistent.
    \item We identify the alignment-realism tension---that forcing motion toward text alignment distorts the motion distribution and hurts generation realism---and propose \textbf{RAG-Diffusion-AR}, which compensates by retrieving real motion priors from the aligned latent bank that bridge text and motion distributions, enabling strong FID and R-Precision simultaneously.
    \item MSA-T2M achieves state-of-the-art performance on HumanML3D-272, providing the first framework that tightly couples multi-scale latent alignment with retrieval-augmented generation as a unified solution to the alignment-realism trade-off.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Text-to-Motion Generation}

T2M methods span two paradigms. Diffusion-based approaches---MotionDiffuse~\citep{zhang2024motiondiffuse}, MDM \citep{tevet2022human}, MLD \citep{chen2023executing}, MoMask \citep{guo2024momask}---apply denoising in pose or latent space; FineMoGen \citep{zhang2023finemogen} and MotionLCM \citep{dai2024motionlcm} further refine controllability and speed.
Despite strong benchmark performance, these text-only methods struggle on compositional descriptions, where the generator must infer plausible motion from language alone.
More recent work couples continuous latent-space generation with causal autoregressive modeling \citep{meng2024rethinking,meng2025absolute,xiao2025motionstreamer,zhu2025motiongpt3,he2025molingo,yu2026causal}.
MotionStreamer \citep{xiao2025motionstreamer}---our closest baseline---encodes motion into a 272-dimensional causal latent space via a CNN-VAE and generates latents autoregressively with a Transformer diffusion head, but optimizes its encoder for reconstruction only, leaving the latent space semantically unstructured.
\emph{Without explicit semantic structure, neither fine-grained alignment nor semantics-based retrieval is possible}---the fundamental gap our work addresses.

\subsection{Motion--Language Alignment in Latent Space}

MotionCLIP \citep{tevet2022motionclip} pioneered cross-modal alignment toward CLIP \citep{pmlr-v139-radford21a} text embeddings; TMR \citep{petrovich2023tmr} extends this with contrastive learning for joint retrieval.
Yet subsequent T2M methods---MDM, MLD, T2M-GPT \citep{zhang2023generating}, MotionStreamer---abandoned explicit latent-space alignment entirely, relying on T5 \citep{raffel2020exploring} or CLIP implicitly, leaving no structural constraint on the latent space.
\citet{li2025unimotion} and \citet{zhang2025kinmo} perform multi-scale alignment in the \emph{raw data space}, improving generation quality but not producing a compact, retrievable latent structure.
Our work is the first to achieve joint local--global multi-scale alignment directly in a continuous latent space and exploit it as the retrieval index for RAG.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Motion}

RAG improves generation by conditioning on retrieved real examples \citep{lewis2020retrieval,gao2023retrieval,blattmann2022retrieval,ren2026videorag}.
In T2M, ReMoDiffuse \citep{zhang2023remodiffuse} and MoRAG \citep{kalakonda2025morag} retrieve raw motion sequences and inject them as dense conditioning, demonstrating that real motion priors improve realism.
However, raw-space retrieval keys are high-dimensional, noisy, and redundant, limiting precision for compositional queries.
Our method instead retrieves in the semantically aligned latent space using \([CLS]\)---aligned to text at both local and global scales---and injects retrieved priors as soft prefix tokens rather than dense per-step conditioning, preserving generative flexibility while benefiting from real motion priors.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Problem Definition and Preliminaries}

Given a natural language description \(y\), our task is to generate a physically plausible and semantically aligned 3D human motion sequence \(M = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \dots, \mathbf{x}_L\}\) by sampling from the conditional distribution \(P(M \mid y)\), where \(\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^D\) denotes the \(i\)-th pose and \(L\) the sequence length.
We adopt the 272-dimensional SMPL parametric representation~\citep{xiao2025motionstreamer}, which directly drives the SMPL model~\citep{loper2023smpl} and bypasses inverse‑kinematics post‑processing.
The core challenge lies in simultaneously achieving two goals: (i) physical realism (natural motion dynamics and body constraints) and (ii) faithful adherence to the textual instruction.

To address this, our pipeline decouples into two stages (see Fig.~\ref{fig:arc}): MSA-VAE (\S\ref{sec:msa-vae}) compresses raw motions into a semantically organized causal latent space via multi‑scale alignment; RAG‑Diffusion‑AR (\S\ref{sec:rag-diffusion}) then generates within this space conditioned on both text and retrieved real motion priors.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Multi-Scale Semantic Alignment VAE }
\label{sec:msa-vae}

While recent works \citep{meng2024rethinking, meng2025absolute, xiao2025motionstreamer} compress motion into low-dimensional causal latent spaces for efficiency, conventional compression focuses solely on geometric reconstruction without explicit semantic structuring---leaving the latent space unorganized for precise text-motion alignment.

Multi-Scale Semantic Alignment VAE addresses this gap by embedding multi-scale semantic alignment directly into a causal
variational autoencoder. It produces two complementary representations:
(1) a sequence of \textbf{local latents} \(\mathbf{z}_{1:n}\) for diffusion-based generation, and
(2) a global semantic feature $[CLS]$ that serves as the retrieval token
for RAG-Diffusion-AR.

\subsubsection{Representation VAE}

Following the causal latent space design of MotionStreamer~\citep{xiao2025motionstreamer}
and MARDM~\citep{meng2024rethinking}, we build the CausalTemporalCNN-VAE, a low-level motion encoder
\(\mathcal{E}_{\text{cnn}}\) and decoder \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{cnn}}\) with 1D causal convolutions
to explicitly model temporal dependencies. Given raw motion
\(\mathbf{X} = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N\}\) with \(\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{272}\),
\(\mathcal{E}_{\text{cnn}}\) applies stacked causal layers with temporal downsampling rate
\(l = 4\) and motion-dimension compression to hidden size \(h\), outputting posterior parameters
\(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \log\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2 \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_c}\) where \(n = N/l\).
A latent variable \(\mathbf{z}\) is sampled via reparameterization, from which
\(\mathcal{D}_{\text{cnn}}\) reconstructs \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}\).

\subsubsection{Semantic Autoencoder}

We feed the deterministic component \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:n}\) into a Transformer encoder
\(\mathcal{E}_{\text{trans}}\) with a prepended learnable \texttt{[CLS]} \citep{devlin2019bert} token;
its output \([CLS] \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}\) serves as the global motion feature.
A Transformer decoder \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{trans}}\) reconstructs \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:n}\) from \([CLS]\),
and reconstruction loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{feature}}\) forces \([CLS]\) to retain full structural information.

\subsubsection{Multi-Scale Semantic Alignment}

We align the latent space at two complementary scales.

\paragraph{Local alignment.}
Using frame-level action labels from BABEL~\citep{punnakkal2021babel}, we construct a local
text embedding \(\mathbf{l}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t}\) for each latent step by averaging the
T5 embeddings of all frame labels within the corresponding temporal window. The local
alignment loss directly acts on the deterministic center \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i\):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{local}} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left(1 - \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \cdot \mathbf{l}_i}
{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_i\| \|\mathbf{l}_i\|}\right).
\label{eq:loss-local}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Global alignment.}
Using the global captions from HumanML3D \citep{Guo_2022_CVPR}, we employ a dynamic interpolation strategy to construct a ``local-motion-aware'' global text target. Because training uses randomly cropped windows of fixed length \(T=64\), while the global caption corresponds to the full long sequence (length \(L\)), direct alignment would cause semantic mismatch. Therefore, we introduce a dynamic interpolation weight \(\alpha = T/L\) and fuse the original global embedding \(\mathbf{g}_{\text{global}}\) with the window-pooled local text \(\mathbf{l}_{\text{pooled}}\):
    \begin{align}
    \mathbf{g}_{\text{mixed}} &= (1-\alpha)\,\mathbf{g}_{\text{global}} + \alpha\,\mathbf{l}_{\text{pooled}}, \label{eq:g-mixed}\\
    \mathbf{g}_{\text{target}} &= \frac{\mathbf{g}_{\text{mixed}}}{\|\mathbf{g}_{\text{mixed}}\|_2}.
    \label{eq:g-target}
    \end{align}
    The global alignment loss then applies cosine similarity between the \([CLS]\) output and the normalized target:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{global}} = 1 - \frac{[CLS] \cdot \mathbf{g}_{\text{target}}}{\|[CLS]\| \, \|\mathbf{g}_{\text{target}}\|}.
    \label{eq:loss-global}
    \end{equation}


This dual-scale design ensures that each local latent aligns with fine-grained action semantics,
while the global token aggregates the compositional semantics of the entire window---forming
an ideal basis for retrieval.

\subsubsection{Three-Stage Progressive Training}

We train MSA-VAE in three stages:
(1) Train \(\mathcal{E}_{\text{cnn}}\) and \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{cnn}}\) with only reconstruction,
KL, and root-joint losses to establish a stable physical latent space.
(2) Freeze the CNN-VAE and train the Transformer autoencoder with latent reconstruction loss
\(\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}} - \boldsymbol{\mu}\|_2^2\) and dual-scale alignment losses
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:loss-local}--\ref{eq:loss-global}).
(3) Jointly fine-tune all components.
Detailed loss formulations are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:loss}.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Autoregressive Diffusion Generation (RAG-Diffusion-AR)}
\label{sec:rag-diffusion}

We adopt a latent-space autoregressive diffusion paradigm~\citep{xiao2025motionstreamer}
but introduce a retrieval augmentation mechanism that explicitly injects real motion priors
into the generation process. The generation is formalized as:
\begin{equation}
p(\mathbf{z}_{1:n} \mid \mathbf{t}, \mathcal{R}) = \prod_{i=1}^{n}
p(\mathbf{z}_i \mid \mathbf{z}_{<i}, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{r}),
\label{eq:ar-factorization}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf{t}\) is the text condition and \(\mathbf{r}\) is the retrieved motion prior.

\subsubsection{Retrieval Prior Database}

Using the global semantic feature \([CLS]\) from MSA-VAE, we pre-encode all
training motions offline to construct a prior bank
\(\mathcal{R} = \{[CLS]^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{K}\).
At inference, given text embedding \(\mathbf{t}\) (from T5-XXL, \(d_t = 768\)), we retrieve the
top-\(K\) most similar samples via cosine similarity. Their features \(\{[CLS]^{(k)}\}\)
and scores \(\{s_k\}\) are fused via softmax-weighted pooling into a single retrieval prior token:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{\exp(s_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\exp(s_j)}
\cdot \text{Proj}([CLS]^{(k)}),
\label{eq:retrieval-fusion}
\end{equation}
where \(\text{Proj}(\cdot)\) maps to the Transformer hidden dimension \(d_{\text{model}} = 768\).

\subsubsection{Autoregressive Diffusion Generation}

At each step \(i\), we concatenate the text token \(\mathbf{t}\), retrieval prior \(\mathbf{r}\),
and previously generated latents \(\mathbf{z}_{<i}\) into a prefix sequence \(\mathbf{s}_i\), which
is fed into a causal Transformer. Its output \(\mathbf{c}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}\)
aggregates all conditioning signals. A lightweight MLP diffusion head then denoises the next
latent: during training, the head predicts the noise \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\) added to the
ground-truth latent \(\mathbf{z}_i\) conditioned on \(\mathbf{c}_i\) and timestep \(t\):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{diff}} = \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}, t}
\big[\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_i^{(t)} \mid t, \mathbf{c}_i)\|_2^2\big].
\label{eq:diff-loss}
\end{equation}
During inference, starting from pure noise, the head iteratively denoises to produce
\(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_i\).

\subsubsection{Synergistic Classifier-Free Guidance}

We adopt CFG \citep{ho2022classifier,chang2023muse}: during training, we jointly drop text and retrieval conditions
with probability 10\%, replacing them with learnable null embeddings \(\emptyset_{\text{text}}, \emptyset_{\text{ret}}\).
At inference, the noise prediction at each step is extrapolated:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{guided}} = \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{uncond}}
+ s \cdot \big(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{cond}} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{uncond}}\big),
\label{eq:cfg}
\end{equation}
where \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{cond}} = \epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_i^{(t)} \mid t, \mathbf{t},
\mathbf{r})\), \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{uncond}} = \epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_i^{(t)} \mid t,
\emptyset_{\text{text}}, \emptyset_{\text{ret}})\), and \(s = 4.0\).

\subsubsection{Inference}

Generation terminates when the distance between the current latent and a pre-computed reference
end latent \(\mathbf{z}_{\text{end}}\) (obtained by encoding an all-zero pose) falls below a
threshold for two consecutive steps. Finally, the MSA-VAE decoder reconstructs the latent
sequence \(\{\hat{\mathbf{z}}_1, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_n\}\) into the full motion frame sequence.





\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Experimental Settings}

\textbf{Dataset.}
We train and evaluate on HumanML3D-272~\citep{xiao2025motionstreamer}, which provides 14,616 motion sequences paired with 44,970 natural language descriptions (avg.\ 3 texts per motion), all resampled to 30\,FPS and represented in the 272-dimensional SMPL parametric format.
For the local alignment supervision in MSA-VAE Stages 2--3, we use frame-level action labels from BABEL~\citep{punnakkal2021babel}: we extract the intersection of HumanML3D and BABEL clips, pool the BABEL frame labels within each fixed-length window of \(T{=}64\) frames, and encode them with T5 to form local text targets \(\mathbf{l}_i\).

\textbf{Evaluation Metrics.}
Following the standard T2M protocol~\citep{Guo_2022_CVPR} with the TMR evaluator \citep{petrovich2023tmr}, we report:
(1)~FID (\(\downarrow\)): Fréchet distance between distributions of generated and real motion features;
(2)~R-Precision (R@1/2/3, \(\uparrow\)): text-to-motion retrieval accuracy measuring semantic alignment;
(3)~MM-Dist (\(\downarrow\)): mean Euclidean distance between motion and text embeddings in the joint space; and
(4)~Diversity (Div, \(\rightarrow\)): average pairwise distance among generated motions, where values closer to real data indicate better coverage.

\textbf{Implementation Details.}
Our framework is implemented in PyTorch and trained on NVIDIA RTX~4090 GPUs.
MSA-VAE: the causal CNN-VAE uses downsampling rate \(l{=}4\) and latent dim \(d_c{=}16\); the semantic Transformer autoencoder has 6 layers, 8 heads, hidden dim 768, and FFN dim 2048; training follows three stages of 2000K / 25K / 5K iterations.
RAG-Diffusion-AR: a LLaMA-style decoder-only Transformer~\citep{touvron2023llama} with 12 layers, 12 heads, hidden size 768; a 9-layer MLP diffusion head with AdaLN~\citep{peebles2023scalable}; cosine noise schedule with \(T_{\text{diff}}{=}50\) steps; CFG dropout 0.1 and guidance scale \(s{=}4.0\).
All stages use AdamW with initial lr \(1{\times}10^{-4}\) and cosine annealing.
Loss weights: \(\lambda_{\text{root}}{=}7.0\), \(\lambda_{\text{latent}}{=}1.0\), \(\lambda_{\text{global}}{=}0.5\), \(\lambda_{\text{local}}{=}0.2\).

\subsection{Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods}

\subsubsection{Quantitative Comparison}

We compare against representative T2M baselines on HumanML3D-272 under the TMR evaluation protocol.
Baselines span three paradigms: (i) latent-space diffusion (MDM~\citep{tevet2022human}, MLD~\citep{chen2023executing}); (ii) discrete-token autoregressive (MoMask~\citep{guo2024momask}, MotionGPT~\citep{jiang2023motiongpt}); (iii) the strongest latent-space autoregressive baseline (MotionStreamer~\citep{xiao2025motionstreamer}); and (iv) the most relevant RAG baseline (ReMoDiffuse~\citep{zhang2023remodiffuse}).


As shown in Tab.~\ref{tab:sota}, MSA-T2M achieves the best FID (10.832) among all methods, improving over the strongest prior latent-space method MotionStreamer by \textbf{8.1\%} and over all non-RAG baselines by a large margin.
In MM-Dist, our method achieves the best score (15.917), surpassing even ReMoDiffuse (15.943), indicating that our generated motions are closer in embedding space to their text descriptions.
Compared to ReMoDiffuse---the most relevant RAG baseline---our method achieves substantially better FID (10.832 vs.\ 13.700) and MM-Dist, while R-Precision is lower (R@1: 0.638 vs.\ 0.718).
We attribute this gap to the fact that ReMoDiffuse retrieves raw motion sequences and injects them directly as conditioning, which biases the model toward high-retrieval-recall outputs at the cost of generation diversity; our method instead retrieves compact latent features, preserving generative freedom while still benefiting from real motion priors.
Overall, MSA-T2M achieves the best balance between motion quality (FID, MM-Dist, Div) and semantic alignment (R-Precision).

\subsection{Ablation Studies}

We conduct ablations to validate each design choice in MSA-T2M.
All ablations use \(K{=}3\) for efficiency; the main SOTA comparison uses \(K{=}5\) (see below).

\textbf{Component ablation.}
Tab.~\ref{tab:ablation} dissects the contributions of local alignment, global alignment, and retrieval augmentation.

\textit{(1) RAG consistently improves motion quality.}
Removing the RAG branch while keeping both alignment losses (``w/o RAG'') increases FID from 10.826 to 11.697 (+0.871, $+$8.1\%).
This confirms that real motion priors from the semantically structured prior bank provide complementary guidance beyond text conditioning alone, and that alignment alone is insufficient to reach the best FID.

\textit{(2) Local alignment is the primary driver of latent space structure.}
Global alignment alone (``w/o local align, w/o RAG'') achieves FID~11.236, which already outperforms the reconstruction-only baseline (MotionStreamer at 11.790), indicating that even coarse global alignment improves the latent space.
However, the variant retaining global alignment and RAG but removing local alignment (``w/o local align'') achieves FID~11.358---worse than our full model by 0.532---demonstrating that frame-level supervision from BABEL is essential for fine-grained semantic structure in the latent space.

\textit{(3) Global alignment amplifies retrieval; full model achieves best FID and Diversity.}
The full model (10.826) outperforms ``w/o local align'' (11.358) because local+global alignment enables \([CLS]\) to capture compositionally-aware semantics and yield more relevant priors; the slight R-Precision dip is expected, as local alignment regularizes toward diverse action semantics, trading retrieval recall for generation realism.



\textbf{Effect of retrieved sample count \textit{K}.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_k} shows that FID improves from \(K{=}1\) to \(K{=}5\) (best FID) but degrades beyond \(K{=}7\), while R@3 remains stable throughout.
This indicates that a moderate number of priors provides sufficient semantic coverage without introducing out-of-distribution noise; we fix \(K{=}3\) for SOTA comparison and ablations.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper addresses the alignment-realism trade-off in text-to-motion generation by proposing MSA-T2M.
The key idea is to treat multi-scale semantic alignment and retrieval-augmented generation as a tightly coupled solution: MSA-VAE structures the latent space into a discriminative retrieval index at both local (frame) and global (sequence) scales, while RAG-Diffusion-AR injects real motion priors to compensate for the realism loss that alignment introduces.
On HumanML3D-272, MSA-T2M achieves state-of-the-art FID (10.826) and competitive R-Precision (R@1: 0.659); ablations confirm that local and global alignment contribute complementarily, and that RAG consistently restores the FID degraded by alignment---validating the tightly coupled design.

Limitations and future directions are discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}.

\section{Human Motion Representation and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{app:representation}

\paragraph{From HumanML3D-263 to MS-272.}
The HumanML3D benchmark~\citep{Guo_2022_CVPR} introduced a 263-dimensional pose representation comprising root trajectory, local joint positions, velocities, joint rotations (recovered via inverse kinematics from AMASS~\citep{mahmood2019amass}), and foot contact labels.
While this representation enabled unified benchmarking, two limitations have emerged.
First, MARDM~\citep{meng2024rethinking} showed that the first 67 dimensions contain most of the information, indicating substantial redundancy.
Second, the IK-recovered joint rotations suffer from twist ambiguity and jitter artifacts; converting generated motions back to SMPL body parameters requires running SMPLify~\citep{bogo2016keep}, which takes $\sim$60 seconds per 10-second clip and introduces additional errors.

MotionStreamer~\citep{xiao2025motionstreamer} addressed both issues by extracting SMPL joint rotations directly from AMASS and encoding them as 6D continuous rotations~\citep{zhou2019continuity}, yielding a \textbf{272-dimensional representation} (MS-272).
Each pose $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{272}$ decomposes as:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Root (8D):} linear velocities $\dot{r}^x, \dot{r}^z \in \mathbb{R}$ on the XZ-plane; 6D root angular velocity $\dot{r}^a \in \mathbb{R}^6$.
    \item \textbf{Joints (264D):} local positions $\mathbf{j}^p \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 22}$, local velocities $\mathbf{j}^v \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 22}$, and 6D local rotations $\mathbf{j}^r \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 22}$ relative to the root, for all $K=22$ SMPL joints.
\end{itemize}
The 6D rotation parameterization is continuous and singularity-free, guarantees consistent bone lengths, and allows generated motions to drive an SMPL character directly without any post-processing.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
Concurrent with the representation redesign, MotionStreamer replaces the original GRU-based feature extractor with \textbf{TMR}~\citep{petrovich2023tmr}---a contrastive text--motion retrieval model---as the evaluation backbone.
TMR produces a more semantically coherent joint embedding space, yielding more reliable FID, R-Precision, and MM-Dist estimates for methods that operate in continuous latent spaces.
The resulting \textbf{MS-272 + TMR} protocol has been adopted by subsequent works~\citep{meng2024rethinking,meng2025absolute,fan2025go} for fair comparison.
We follow this protocol throughout all experiments.

\section{Full Loss Formulation}
\label{app:loss}

The total MSA-VAE training objective combines six terms:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}
+ \lambda_{\text{root}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{root}}
+ \lambda_{\text{latent}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{latent}}
+ \lambda_{\text{global}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{global}}
+ \lambda_{\text{local}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{local}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$.}
Following the $\sigma$-VAE formulation~\citep{rybkin2021simple}, we use an optimal-$\sigma$ Gaussian negative log-likelihood:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} = \sum_{d=1}^{D}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left(\frac{(x_{di}-\hat{x}_{di})^2}{2\sigma^{*2}} + \ln\sigma^{*}\right),
\quad
\sigma^{*2} = \frac{1}{DN}\sum_{d,i}(x_{di}-\hat{x}_{di})^2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{KL divergence $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}$.}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{d_c}\sum_{i'=1}^{n}
\bigl(\mu_{di'}^2 + \sigma_{di'}^2 - \ln\sigma_{di'}^2 - 1\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $(\mu_{di'}, \sigma_{di'})$ are the posterior parameters of the $i'$-th causal latent in the $d$-th dimension.

\paragraph{Root-joint loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{root}}$.}
The same NLL is applied to the root dimensions only ($D_{\text{root}}=8$, the first 8 dims of the 272-dim representation), weighted by $\lambda_{\text{root}}=7.0$ to stabilize root trajectory reconstruction:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{root}} = \sum_{d=1}^{D_{\text{root}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left(\frac{(x_{di}-\hat{x}_{di})^2}{2\sigma^{*2}} + \ln\sigma^{*}\right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Latent reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{latent}}$.}
The Transformer decoder $\mathcal{D}_{\text{trans}}$ reconstructs local CNN latents $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:n}$ from $[CLS]$; a mean-squared reconstruction loss is applied:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{feature}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_i\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
with weight $\lambda_{\text{latent}}=1.0$.

\paragraph{Global alignment loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{global}}$.}
As defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:loss-global}, cosine similarity between $[CLS]$ and the dynamically interpolated text target $\mathbf{g}_{\text{target}}$, with weight $\lambda_{\text{global}}=0.5$.

\paragraph{Local alignment loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{local}}$.}
As defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:loss-local}, average cosine similarity between each local latent $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ and its corresponding BABEL frame-label embedding $\mathbf{l}_i$, with weight $\lambda_{\text{local}}=0.2$.

\section{Detailed Architecture}
\label{app:arch}

\subsection{Representation VAE)}

The Representation VAE follows the Causal TAE design of MotionStreamer~\citep{xiao2025motionstreamer}.
Both encoder $\mathcal{E}_{\text{cnn}}$ and decoder $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cnn}}$ are built from 1D causal ResNet blocks~\citep{he2016deep}.
Causality is enforced by left-only padding: for a convolution with kernel size $k_t$, stride $s_t$, and dilation $d_t$, we pad $(k_t-1)\times d_t + (1-s_t)$ frames at the \emph{beginning} of the sequence, so each output frame depends only on past frames.

The encoder applies two successive downsampling stages (strided causal convolution, rate $2\times$ each), yielding total temporal compression $l=4$.
Each downsampling stage is followed by a causal ResNet block with three parallel dilated residual branches (dilations 9, 3, 1) operating at hidden size $h=1024$.
The final encoder output is projected to $2d_c=32$ channels to produce posterior parameters $(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \log\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 16}$, where $n=N/4$ and $d_c=16$.
A linear adapter further reduces the sampled latents to the compact space used for downstream generation.
The decoder mirrors the encoder, replacing strided downsampling with nearest-neighbour upsampling.

\subsection{Semantic Autoencoder}

The Semantic Autoencoder consists of a Transformer encoder $\mathcal{E}_{\text{trans}}$ and a Transformer decoder $\mathcal{D}_{\text{trans}}$, both built on standard multi-head self-attention blocks.
A learnable \texttt{[CLS]} token is prepended to the sequence of local CNN latents $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:n}$ before $\mathcal{E}_{\text{trans}}$.
The \texttt{[CLS]} output is projected by a linear head to the text embedding dimension (768) to form the global retrieval feature $[CLS]$.
Each local latent output from $\mathcal{E}_{\text{trans}}$ is similarly projected by a separate linear head for local alignment.
$\mathcal{D}_{\text{trans}}$ takes $[CLS]$ as memory and autoregressively reconstructs $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{1:n}$ via cross-attention.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{app:limitations}

\paragraph{Frame-level supervision coverage.}
Local alignment relies on BABEL, which covers only atomic action categories (e.g., ``walk'', ``sit'') and lacks body-part-level annotations, limiting fine-grained instruction following for body-part control. Extending to richly annotated data and finer-grained alignment is a natural next step.

\paragraph{Single-segment generation.}
The current model supports offline single-segment generation; applying multi-scale alignment to online streaming generation for arbitrary-length motion is an important open direction.
